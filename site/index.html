    How good is the forecast?

    Every number below is out-of-sample. Each year is predicted by a model fitted without that year (leave-one-year-out cross-validation), and every forecast is compared with simply guessing the average date.

    Method

    Observed bloom dates. For each region, tree group and year, I take the median date of iNaturalist observations of that species in its flowering window, excluding photos annotated as "no flowers". People photograph cherries when they're in bloom, so the median observation tracks peak bloom.

    Weather. Environment and Climate Change Canada daily temperatures. Each region's station chain is joined together after a month-by-month bias adjustment over the years the stations overlap. Greater Victoria runs from Gonzales Heights (1898) to Gonzales CS.

    Models. Each group is tested with four candidate models:

    • Climatology: the average bloom date for the region.
    • Thermal time: bloom when growing degree-days above a base temperature reach a threshold.
    • Chill-adjusted thermal time: as above, but warmer winters with less chill need more spring warmth.
    • Temperature-sensitivity regression: bloom date regressed on the average temperature over a late-winter window.

    Parameters are pooled across regions, so the less-observed Island regions borrow strength from Victoria and Vancouver. The model with the lowest cross-validated error wins.

    Forecasts. Observed weather to date, followed by each of the last 30 years' weather for the rest of the season (an analog ensemble). The model's cross-validated errors are then added on, so the ranges reflect the model's real accuracy.
